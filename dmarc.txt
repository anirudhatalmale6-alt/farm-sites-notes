DMARC RECORDS - name.com
========================

Copy the values from this page, not from the Freelancer chat window. Chat turns
certain character pairs into emoji, which is what broke the first attempt.


1. MEADOWANDMOOR.CO.UK - ALREADY ADDED, NEEDS ONE SMALL CORRECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
You've got this in already and the start of it is spot on. The only thing wrong
is the address at the end - it currently sends the reports to the
FarmTradeDirect mailbox instead of the Meadow & Moor one.

You don't need to retype the whole thing. Click Edit on that record, and in the
Answer box change just the part after the @ symbol, so that:

   contact@farmtradedirect.com

becomes:

   contact@meadowandmoor.co.uk

Leave everything else exactly as it is. Save.

Why it matters rather than being fussiness: a domain can only send its reports
to a different domain's mailbox if that other domain publishes a record giving
permission. Without it the reports get refused and quietly go nowhere. Pointing
each domain at its own mailbox avoids the whole problem.

The finished record should read:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:contact@meadowandmoor.co.uk

NOTES
-----
The Host field is just _dmarc on its own. name.com adds the domain after it for
you - that's the greyed out text under the box.

Leave the Priority field alone, it doesn't apply to TXT records.

Do not edit or delete any record that's already there. Only add new ones, and
the one edit described above.

"p=none" means monitor only - it reports problems without blocking any mail.
That's deliberate. Once the sites have been sending live for a few weeks and
nothing legitimate is failing, we can tighten it.
